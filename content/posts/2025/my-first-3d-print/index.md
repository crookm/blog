Been feeling like I wanna get into the Maker's mindset and play around with 3D printing, and there was a deal on a small 3D printer at The Store, so I got one and now I'm trying it out!

The 3D printer I got is a Bambu Lab A1 Mini. It's small and proprietary, but has a good entry-level price and a whole lot of sensors and such which I've come to learn are important for good quality prints (i.e. auto-levelling of the bed, flow sensing etc). If I get real into it, I may get a bigger one with an open ecosystem - but this one should be great for me to start with.

Of course my first print is a [3DBenchy](https://github.com/CreativeTools/3DBenchy), as is required by law (/s).

The first one I tried was just using the free filament they give you in the box with the printer; it came-out great! The lines were noticable (resolution?), but the quality was excellent compared to some printed pieces I had seen just a few years ago - the technology has come very far very quickly!

I also took a video of it while it was printing and when I showed my friends, all of them asked if it was a timelapse because of how fast it was. Some didn't believe me when I said it was realtime!

{{< video src="white-benchy-printing" caption="Look at this speed racer - this is truly realtime!" muted="true" autoplay="true" loop="true" >}}

After getting some more filament, matte black PLA in this case, I did another print of the benchy. This time, I tried with a much finer layer size of 0.08mm. I'm not sure if my nozzle even supports this properly but I did get a much higher resolution print with barely perceivable layer lines.

{{< figure src="black-benchy.jpg" caption="My second print, a matte black 3D benchy with finer layer thickness." alt="Picture of a matte black 3D printed boat figure, with barely noticeable print lines in the layering, but some wispy lines on the inner parts of the print." >}}

Again, I also took a video, but using the printer's camera to do a timelapse. The camera quality is terrible likely because of the lighting. According to the Bambu Lab wiki the brightness is locked when the print starts, so this may have been because I started the job just before evening and it got dark out?

{{< video src="black-benchy-timelapse" caption="The timelapse as captured by the printer itself, kinda terrible on account of it barely being visible. You can see the plastic bits getting flung about though." muted="true" autoplay="true" loop="true" >}}

Not sure if it was because of the timelapse or the layer height, but there were little bits of plastic strewn about my workspace. Maybe it was a combination of the two, as when you enable the timelapse in the software without a "prime tower" (whatever that is) the software makes the printer head move out of the way after each layer to allow a picture to be taken, leaving strands of thin plastic to be blown around.

{{< figure src="slicer-travel-visualisation.png" caption="The printer head travel visualisation of the slicer software" alt="Screenshot of 3D printer slicer software, showing a visualisation of the movement of the printer head with blue lines - which show a very clear movement pattern beyond the object being printed." >}}

Regardless, with a bit of cleanup, I'm happy with both results! Having a look at some of the things on thingiverse, and some ideas I have myself, I think this is somehting I'll be very interested in continuing with.
